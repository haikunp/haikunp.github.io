## The short version

OpenAI is not a normal company. It is a nonprofit foundation that controls a for-profit subsidiary, and that structure is the key to understanding who "owns" it.

## Who actually controls it

The **OpenAI Nonprofit** (a 501(c)(3) foundation) is the controlling entity. It appoints the board that governs the whole organization and holds the ultimate mission — ensuring that artificial general intelligence benefits all of humanity.

Beneath the nonprofit sits **OpenAI Global, LLC**, a "capped-profit" entity. Investors in this arm can earn returns, but those returns are capped, and the nonprofit keeps control.

## Where Microsoft fits

Microsoft is OpenAI's largest strategic investor, with a reported commitment of around $13 billion. That investment buys compute and profit participation — **not ownership or board control**. Microsoft does not own OpenAI, and it cannot direct the company.

## What about the 2025 restructuring?

OpenAI has discussed restructuring its for-profit arm into a **public benefit corporation (PBC)** to raise capital more easily. Under the plans reported, the nonprofit would continue to control the PBC, and its mission would remain the governing mandate. The exact details have shifted over time, so treat restructuring headlines as evolving.

## Why the structure matters

The nonprofit control layer is designed to stop commercial pressure from overriding safety and mission commitments. Whether it achieves that is a matter of ongoing debate among researchers, investors and regulators.
