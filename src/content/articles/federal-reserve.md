## The short answer

The **Federal Reserve** ("the Fed") is the central bank of the United States. It has three core jobs:

1. **Monetary policy** — promoting maximum employment and stable prices.
2. **Bank supervision** — regulating banks to keep them safe and sound.
3. **Financial stability** — helping prevent and contain financial crises.

## Its main tool: interest rates

The Fed's most visible lever is the **federal funds rate**, the rate banks charge each other for overnight loans. By raising or lowering it, the Fed influences borrowing costs across the economy.

- **Higher rates** tend to cool inflation by making borrowing more expensive.
- **Lower rates** tend to stimulate spending and investment.

## Why it matters to you

Fed decisions affect mortgage rates, credit cards, savings yields, the stock market and the dollar. That is why its meetings and statements are closely watched.

## Independence and accountability

The Fed is designed to be independent of short-term political pressure, while still reporting to Congress. Its chair and governors are appointed by the president and confirmed by the Senate.
