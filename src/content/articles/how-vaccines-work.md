## The short answer

A vaccine gives your immune system a **safe preview** of a pathogen. Your body responds by building antibodies and memory cells. If you later meet the real germ, those memory cells mount a fast, effective defense.

## The main types

- **Inactivated vaccines** use a killed version of the pathogen.
- **Live-attenuated vaccines** use a weakened version that does not cause disease in healthy people.
- **Subunit vaccines** use a specific protein or piece of the pathogen.
- **mRNA vaccines** deliver genetic instructions so your cells temporarily produce a key protein, which the immune system learns to recognize.

## Why they work so well

The immune system has **memory**. After vaccination, memory B and T cells persist. On reinfection, they respond faster and stronger than a first-time response — often preventing illness or making it milder.

## Common questions

**Do vaccines cause the disease?** Inactivated and subunit vaccines cannot. Live-attenuated vaccines are designed to be too weak to cause disease in healthy people.

**How long does protection last?** It varies by vaccine and disease. Some need boosters.

**Are they safe?** Vaccines are tested in clinical trials and monitored after approval. Serious side effects are rare, and health agencies continuously track safety data.
